Situation

Growth in scrolling. Flat growth in connection.

S

NorthStar Social's feed had gotten very good at keeping people scrolling — autoplay, infinite feed, high watch time. What it hadn't grown was Meaningful Social Interactions (MSI): comments, replies, shares, DMs — the person-to-person activity leadership had named a top-line KPI, the same tension Meta described publicly around its 2018 News Feed changes. The Feed team built Ranking v2, a model that re-orders the feed to surface content more likely to spark a reply, not just a scroll.

Constraint 01

It's a social graph, not i.i.d. users

Users are friends with other users in the experiment. A control user can still see the downstream effects of a treated friend's extra comments and shares in their own feed — a classic SUTVA violation that biases naive A/B tests toward zero.

Constraint 02

Launch-week numbers lie

Past launches at NorthStar had shown big day-one lifts that quietly decayed as novelty wore off. Shipping on a day-one number had burned the team before.

Constraint 03

An average can hide harm

A positive topline effect can still mean a vulnerable segment — low-activity, high churn-risk users — is quietly being made worse off by the same change.

Task

Four questions the launch decision hinged on

T

Before Ranking v2 could go to 100% of users, the experimentation had to survive scrutiny on every axis that had burned the team before — not just clear a p-value bar.

01

Is the effect real? Not just statistically significant by a naive test, but significant once inference correctly accounts for how treatment was actually assigned.

02

Is it durable? Or is the launch-week number a novelty effect that will fade once the initial buzz wears off — and if so, what's the real steady-state lift to plan around?

03

Who benefits, and who's hurt? A single average effect can conceal a segment that gets nothing, or worse, loses retention.

04

What does it cost? Guardrails — time spent (ad inventory) and next-day retention — had to move in a direction leadership could defend.

Action

A ten-step pipeline, in the order it had to run

A

Each step below depends on the one before it — sanity checks gate the effect estimate, the effect estimate gates the variance-reduction step, and so on. This is the actual order the analysis pipeline executes in analysis.py.

01Design

Randomize by friend-cluster, not by user

200 friend-clusters (~60 users each) were randomized whole, 50/50, instead of randomizing individual users. This is the standard fix for network interference in social-graph experiments — it keeps a treated user's friends from contaminating the control group.

cluster randomizationSUTVA-safe
02Check

Verify the randomization before trusting anything downstream

A sample-ratio-mismatch test on cluster assignment and t-tests on every pre-period covariate ran first. A broken randomization invalidates every result that follows it.

SRM χ² testpre-period balance
03Estimate

Run the naive analysis on purpose — to show why it's wrong

A plain OLS regression ignoring clustering returned a standard error of SE = 0.101. Since treatment was assigned by cluster, this understates uncertainty through pseudo-replication — 60 correlated "observations" per cluster masquerading as independent data.

04Correct

Cluster-robust standard errors

Re-run with SEs clustered on the actual randomization unit. The honest standard error came out SE = 0.248 — 2.4× larger. The effect held up, but this is the number that belongs in the launch decision doc, not the naive one.

05Sharpen

CUPED variance reduction

Using each user's pre-period MSI as a covariate cut residual variance by 98.4% — equivalent to running the experiment on ~63× as many users, at zero additional cost.

pre-period covariate
06Decompose

Trace the novelty-decay curve, day by day

A separate diff-in-diff treatment-effect estimate for each of the 14 post-launch days showed the lift falling from +2.47 → +1.08 — a 56% decay — with the trailing 5-day average converging to a steady-state +1.15 MSI/user/day.

07Segment

Heterogeneous treatment effects, two ways

Subgroup ATEs by activity tier and a T-learner (two random forests, treated vs. control) were run independently and cross-checked against each other — agreement between two different methods is what makes a segment story trustworthy rather than a modeling artifact.

interaction regressionT-learner CATE
08Guard

CUPED-adjust the guardrails too

The pre-period balance check flagged a chance imbalance in time-spent between arms — expected noise with only 200 randomization clusters. CUPED-correcting the guardrail the same way as the primary metric reversed the raw (misleading) reading.

09Stress-test

Quantify what individual randomization would have cost

A companion dataset simulated the same experiment with individual- instead of cluster-level randomization and an explicit friend-spillover mechanism, then analyzed it the naive way — to measure, not just assert, the value of the design choice in step 01.

interference bias demo
10Translate

Turn effect sizes into a number a VP can act on

Steady-state per-user effects were scaled against an illustrative 450M DAU base to size the topline gain and the guardrail cost in absolute daily terms.

GUARDRAIL

Time spent almost got misread. The raw pre/post comparison showed a small, non-significant increase — but a chance pre-period imbalance between arms (expected noise with only 200 clusters) was masking the real effect. CUPED-adjusting this guardrail exactly like the primary metric reversed the reading:

Raw: +0.19 min/day (p = 0.81, not significant) → CUPED-adjusted: −0.82 min/day (p < 0.001, significant)

Business impact, at scale

  • +516M / day incremental meaningful interactions platform-wide, at the verified steady-state effect (+1.15/user) on an illustrative 450M DAU base
  • −367M min / day time-spent cost — a real ad-inventory trade-off, invisible in the raw (uncorrected) guardrail read
  • −1.4M returns / day projected next-day-return shortfall in the dormant segment alone if shipped to them unmodified

What shipped

  1. Ship to power and casual segments. The effect is large and holds up under cluster-robust, CUPED-adjusted inference — and it's durable after novelty decay.
  2. Hold back the dormant segment. Zero MSI benefit plus a real retention cost means launching to them trades away the most fragile users for no upside.
  3. Size the next experiment on +1.15, not +2.47. Pre-registering success thresholds on the steady-state number keeps the team from chasing a novelty artifact again.
  4. Escalate the time-spent trade-off explicitly. Small per-user, material at platform scale — and easy to miss without the CUPED correction.
  5. Keep cluster randomization as the default for any feature with plausible peer effects — the interference demo shows a ~30% understatement risk from getting this wrong.
