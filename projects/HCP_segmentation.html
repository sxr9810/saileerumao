Turning HCP Prescription Data Into a Targeting Strategy

A data-driven HCP segmentation project for Lo Loestrin Fe designed to identify high-value healthcare providers and focus commercial resources where prescription opportunity was greatest.

Key finding: the top 20% of HCPs accounted for approximately 65% of addressable prescriptions.

S

Situation

The pharmaceutical commercial team needed to improve the efficiency of its HCP targeting strategy for Lo Loestrin Fe.

The HCP population represented a broad range of prescription activity, meaning that providers did not contribute equally to the overall addressable prescription opportunity.

A broad targeting strategy risked allocating sales and marketing resources across HCPs with substantially different levels of commercial opportunity.

The core business question:
How can prescription data be used to identify the HCPs with the greatest commercial opportunity and prioritize limited sales resources accordingly?
T

Task

My objective was to develop a data-driven HCP segmentation and prioritization framework that would allow the commercial team to:

  • Identify the highest-value HCPs based on prescription contribution.
  • Quantify how prescription opportunity was distributed across the HCP population.
  • Create actionable HCP priority groups.
  • Determine where sales and marketing resources could have the greatest potential impact.
  • Translate the analytical findings into a practical targeting strategy.
The objective was not simply to segment HCPs. The objective was to determine whether the segmentation could identify a smaller group of HCPs representing a disproportionate share of prescription opportunity.
A

Action

1. Analyzed HCP-level prescription behavior

I analyzed HCP-level prescription data to understand how prescription activity was distributed across the provider population.

Instead of assuming that every HCP represented the same level of opportunity, I examined the concentration of prescription volume across providers.

2. Applied A-priori segmentation

I used an A-priori segmentation approach to rank HCPs based on prescription contribution and divide the population into deciles.

This converted a broad HCP population into a structured hierarchy that made differences in commercial opportunity easier to identify and act upon.

Priority Tier 1

High-value HCPs

Highest prescription-contributing HCPs representing the greatest concentration of addressable opportunity.

Priority Tier 2

Growth HCPs

Mid-tier providers with meaningful prescription activity and potential for additional commercial engagement.

Priority Tier 3

Lower-priority HCPs

Long-tail providers where intensive sales engagement could have comparatively lower expected impact.

3. Quantified prescription concentration

I evaluated the cumulative share of addressable prescriptions captured as progressively larger portions of the HCP population were included.

Top 20% HCP population
~65% Addressable prescriptions captured
~3.25× Approximate concentration relative to an even 20% share

Why this finding mattered

If prescription opportunity were evenly distributed, the top 20% of HCPs would account for roughly 20% of prescriptions.

Instead, the top 20% accounted for approximately 65% of addressable prescriptions.

This indicated substantial concentration of commercial opportunity and provided quantitative evidence for prioritizing the highest-value HCP segment.

4. Converted segmentation into a targeting framework

I translated the decile analysis into actionable priority tiers so that commercial resources could be aligned with the relative opportunity represented by each group.

Segment Analytical Role Commercial Action
High Priority Highest prescription contribution Concentrate sales and marketing resources
Growth Opportunity Meaningful but lower prescription contribution Target selectively for incremental growth
Lower Priority Lower prescription contribution Use lower-intensity or broader-touch strategies
R

Result

The segmentation demonstrated that prescription opportunity was highly concentrated within a relatively small portion of the HCP population.

The most important finding was that approximately 20% of HCPs accounted for ~65% of addressable prescriptions.

Business Impact

~20–30%

improvement in targeting efficiency

The segmentation provided a quantitative basis for focusing commercial resources on HCPs representing the greatest prescription opportunity, improving the efficiency of the targeting strategy by approximately 20–30%.

What changed for the business?

Before

  • Broad HCP population
  • Limited differentiation in targeting
  • Resources distributed across providers with different levels of prescription opportunity

After

  • HCPs ranked using prescription contribution
  • Clear priority tiers
  • Resources aligned with quantified commercial opportunity
Business takeaway: The analysis transformed prescription data into an actionable prioritization strategy, allowing the commercial team to focus disproportionately on the HCPs responsible for the largest share of addressable prescription opportunity.

The Story in One Sentence

I used HCP-level prescription data and A-priori segmentation to identify concentration of commercial opportunity, finding that the top 20% of HCPs represented approximately 65% of addressable prescriptions, which enabled a prioritized targeting strategy and contributed to an estimated 20–30% improvement in targeting efficiency.
